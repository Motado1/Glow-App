What you're actually doing

Right now the app keeps everything on the one device it was typed into. Farms you enter on your laptop don't reach Dan's phone, and photos Dan takes don't reach you. There's nothing in the middle.

Supabase is the thing in the middle — a hosted database, a filing cabinet on the internet that all your devices talk to. Once it's connected:

  • You enter a farm once and the whole crew sees it.
  • Photos upload from the field instead of sitting on a phone.
  • Everyone gets a real login with a real password.
  • You can lock fields so photographers and installers never see them — customer contacts, contracts, anything you decide is yours only. That's the part that genuinely cannot be done today, because everything lives on the device, and anything on the device can be read.
  • Nothing is lost if a phone breaks or a browser gets cleared.

You're creating the account and handing over two values. I do the rest.

01

Create your account

  1. Go to supabase.com.
  2. Click Start your project, top right.
  3. Sign up with GitHub, or with an email and password — either is fine.
  4. Confirm the email if it asks.

Use an address that outlives any one person. This account will eventually own Glow's field data. Tied to a personal Gmail, whoever owns that Gmail owns the data. jared@glow.org is the right kind of choice.

02

Create the organization

First time in, it asks you to make one.

  • Name: Glow
  • Type: Company
  • Plan: Free

Free is genuinely enough to start: 500 MB of database and 1 GB of file storage. The database only holds text — farms, statuses, assignments — and you'd need tens of thousands of farms to fill 500 MB.

Photo storage runs out first. At roughly 1–2 MB per photo after the app compresses it, 1 GB is somewhere around 500–1,000 photos. Fine for a pilot; not enough for a season. The next tier is $25/month and lifts storage to 100 GB — don't pay for it yet. Start free and we'll watch the number.

03

Create the project

  1. Click New project.
  2. Name: glow-field-ops
  3. Database Password: click Generate a password, then copy it and save it somewhere you won't lose — a password manager, or written down somewhere safe.
  4. Region: whichever is closest to where the crew actually works. For Colorado and Kansas, East US (Ohio) or West US (North California). Either is fine.
  5. Click Create new project.

Two things worth knowing. You will almost certainly never need that database password — but it can't be recovered, only reset, which takes the database offline for a minute. Save it once, now. And the region only changes speed by fractions of a second, but it can't be changed later without rebuilding the project — which is the only reason it's worth a moment's thought.

The project takes about two minutes to build. Leave the tab open.

The one thing that matters

Never send the service_role key

On that same screen there's a third key labelled service_role, usually behind a warning and a "reveal" button. Not to me, not to anyone, not in a screenshot.

The difference, without jargon: the anon key is designed to be public — it ships inside the app on every phone. On its own it does nothing; it only lets someone knock on the door, and the rules I set up decide what they can see once inside. A photographer holding it still can't read your customer contacts.

The service_role key skips every rule. Anyone holding it can read, change or delete everything, from anywhere, with no login. It exists for servers, not people.

If you ever paste it somewhere by accident, go back to that API settings page and rotate it. That instantly makes the old one useless — no harm done. Rule of thumb: if a key is hidden behind a "reveal" button, it isn't meant to leave that page.

What changes, and what doesn't

Once it's connected

  • Real logins and passwords for the whole crew.
  • Farms, assignments and statuses sync across devices in seconds.
  • Photos upload from the field and are backed up off the phone.
  • Permission rules enforced on the server — a photographer's app can't even request data it isn't allowed, no matter what someone does to the app.
  • A lost phone loses nothing.

Still won't do

  • Connect to the Glow Hub. PTO stays a button you press.
  • Upload without signal — photos still queue on the phone and send when the crew's back in range. That's already how it works, and it's correct.
  • Rescue anything typed into the app before this is connected. So don't enter your real farm list twice — either wait, or treat the first pass as practice.
